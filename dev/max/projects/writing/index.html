Writing is essential to me. Not because I wake up every morning wanting to write. Because when I am truly awake, I have to.

01 / awake enough to sing

In Sister Act 2, Whoopi Goldberg tells Lauryn Hill’s character: “If you wake up in the morning and you can’t think of anything but singing, then you’re supposed to be a singer, girl.”

I do not wake up every morning thinking I have to sing. But I do. In the sense of being truly awake, I have to sing. For me, singing is writing.

Bill Corbett was a poet, a teacher, the founder of Pressed Wafer, a mentor to me, and a real center of literary life in Boston before he moved to New York. For years, he religiously mailed me books of poetry and prose I never would have found on my own. The gatherings with other writers he invited me into were transformative.

Some sentences stay because they name something you did not know could be named. In eighth grade, dressed for a semi-formal, I squatted in the backyard to pick flowers before leaving for the dance. My mother looked at me and said, “Wow, you have such an elegant neck.” What a strange compliment. I remember it even now.

“A single utterance.”

Bill once said in a workshop that reading my work was like taking in a single utterance. Language propelled by one breath, force carrying through the breaks.

“Poets write in dribs and drabs of time.”

When I worried about my commitment to the art, Bill assured me that very few poets in history had really done it full-time. It’s like capitalism, am I right?

An interactive accordion book designed by artist Leeanne Maxey with poetry by me. It grew out of a mutual love for the fungal and fermenting: the many shapes and textures of fungi, and the metaphors and meanings they bring forth.
