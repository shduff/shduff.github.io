2009–now · Somerville, Massachusetts

Powderhouse

Powderhouse Studios is a small, full-time, tuition-free lab school for teens looking to learn differently.

01 / the short history

Powderhouse is the organization. We started in 2009 as sprout & co., running afternoon and evening programs for youth and adults.

We became a public makerspace and a volunteer-run community education organization. Today, Powderhouse runs Powderhouse Studios. The fuller organizational story belongs on the Powderhouse website.

02 / real food in the play kitchen

Real food in the play kitchen.

When children are very young, they go through the motions. They stir an empty pot. They hand you a plastic egg and watch you pretend to eat it. Partly this is because the full kitchen is too big: the counters are high, the knives are sharp, the stove will burn their face.

That does not make the play kitchen useless or fake. It is a rehearsal space scaled to a small body. But eventually you bring the child into the actual kitchen. You add a stool, a butter knife, one clear job, and a thoughtful adult who is paying enough attention to know when to intervene.

Then you keep changing the parameters. The knife gets sharper. The recipe gets harder. The meal is for people who are actually hungry. The adult steps back, then forward, then back again. The kitchen becomes more real as discernment and skill grow.

the transition mattersThe point is not to make school more like life. The point is to stop treating young people’s lives as rehearsal.

School often forgets the transition. The knobs stay disconnected; the plastic egg gets a rubric. A task can look authentic and still be sealed off from consequence, audience, dependence, and use.

At Powderhouse, real work comes into reach by degrees. Young people use serious tools, make things they care about, depend on other people, and make promises other people can depend on. Adults do not disappear. They arrange the conditions, notice what is happening, offer discernment, change the parameters, and help decide when another training wheel can come off.

03 / when the rubber hits the road

People, not programs

Progressive education is often right about motivation and strangely quiet about Tuesday afternoon. Choice matters. Projects matter. Neither one calls the landlord, finds psychiatric care, sorts out documentation status, or stays close when a young person is having a truly terrible month.

Schools cannot solve every condition shaping a life. A loving adult cannot either. But research on resilience keeps finding a stubborn fact: children who do well through serious hardship commonly have at least one stable, committed relationship with a supportive adult. The lesson is not to turn love into a program metric. It is to make room for people to know one another.

We need institutions that trust youth, and that also trust the adults inside them to improvise: to bend time, change a plan, make a call, hold a boundary, notice the actual need, and respond to the person standing there. Humane institutions should be sturdy without making everyone inside them rigid.

Powderhouse is a school, a community, a workplace, and a laboratory. Those are not separate programs. They are different dimensions of a place trying to support people to be the people they want to be and live the lives they want to live.

Illich called tools convivial when they enlarged people’s capacity for autonomous, creative life together. That feels like the civic project underneath the educational one: building humane institutions in which we can relearn how to be together, work together, and act collectively. Knowing your neighbors—and knowing that their experience of the world may be nothing like yours—is not a soft extra. It is the ground from which a community can notice, tend, argue, and care.
